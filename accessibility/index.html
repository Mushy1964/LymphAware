Accessibility

Designed to be clear and accessible.

LymphAware is being developed so that information can be accessed and understood as easily as possible by the people who use the service.

Accessibility from the beginning

Accessibility is being considered throughout the development of the LymphAware website, patient portal and QR-linked patient profile.

The aim is to create a service that is straightforward to navigate, easy to read and practical for people with different abilities, needs and levels of confidence with technology.

Accessibility should be part of how LymphAware is designed rather than something added after the service has been built.

Simple and understandable

LymphAware is being designed around clear language, consistent layouts and straightforward navigation.

Information should be presented in a way that helps people find what they need without unnecessary complexity.

What we are designing for

Accessibility considerations are being incorporated into the LymphAware design as the website and patient portal develop.

Readable content

Text, headings and spacing are being designed to make information easier to read and understand across different screen sizes.

Clear navigation

Pages should use predictable navigation, meaningful headings and consistent layouts so people can find information easily.

Good contrast

Colours and text should provide sufficient contrast while remaining consistent with the LymphAware green and blue identity.

Keyboard use

Interactive parts of the service should be usable without requiring a mouse wherever practical.

Assistive technology

The website and portal will be developed with appropriate support for screen readers and other assistive technologies in mind.

Mobile-first design

LymphAware is being designed to work clearly on mobile phones, tablets and larger screens.

The QR-linked patient profile

Accessibility is particularly important when someone scans a LymphAware card.

The patient profile is being designed so important information can be understood quickly on a mobile phone without complicated navigation or unnecessary detail.

Accessibility testing

LymphAware is still in development and the website and patient portal will continue to be reviewed as the project progresses.

Accessibility testing will form part of the preparation for the patient pilot so that practical barriers can be identified and improved before wider use.

The pilot will also provide an opportunity to learn directly from people using LymphAware in real situations.

If something is difficult to use

Accessibility problems are sometimes easier to identify through real use than during development alone.

LymphAware intends to provide a straightforward way for people to report accessibility difficulties or request assistance.

Contact arrangements will be added as the service develops towards the patient pilot.

Continuous improvement

Accessibility will be treated as an ongoing responsibility rather than a one-off exercise.

Feedback from patients and other users will help identify improvements as LymphAware develops.

Easy to find. Easy to read. Easy to understand.

LymphAware should remain simple and accessible because people may need to use it at moments when clear communication matters most.
